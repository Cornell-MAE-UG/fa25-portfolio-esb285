## Project Overview 

For the Mechanics of Materials (MAE 3270) course, the final project was to design a wrench that fulfilled a multitude of design criteria using hand calculations as well as ANSYS to do the analysis. The design needed to meet several safety factors while maintaining practical dimensions for real-world use.

### Objectives
The primary design objectives were to create a torque‑measuring wrench capable of safely withstanding applied loads while producing a measurable strain output suitable for a bonded strain gauge. The design was required to meet explicit mechanical criteria: a factor of safety of at least 4 against yielding, 2 against crack growth, 1.5 against fatigue failure, and a minimum strain output of 1 mV/V at the gauge location. Additional objectives included selecting a material that satisfied these constraints, developing a geometry that minimized stress concentrations, and validating the design through both analytical calculations and finite element analysis.

### Process
The design process began with hand calculations using beam theory to estimate stress, strain, displacement, and safety factors for an initial wrench geometry. MATLAB scripts were used to automate these calculations and verify that the preliminary dimensions met the required performance criteria. The wrench was then modeled in Autodesk Fusion 360, where iterative CAD refinement introduced fillets and optimized proportions to reduce stress concentrations. Material selection was performed by comparing required mechanical properties to available alloys, ultimately identifying Aluminum‑2424‑T3 as a suitable and cost‑effective choice. The design was subsequently evaluated in ANSYS using multiple mesh densities and two boundary‑condition configurations (unclamped and clamped fillets). Strain, stress, displacement, and strain‑gauge output were extracted from each simulation to assess structural behavior and confirm analytical predictions.

### Outcomes
The design outcomes showed that the initial hand calculations predicted acceptable stress levels, adequate safety factors, and a strain output exceeding the 1 mV/V requirement. The selected material met all minimum mechanical property thresholds for yield strength, fracture toughness, fatigue strength, and stiffness. Finite element simulations demonstrated consistent trends: mesh refinement increased the accuracy of strain and stress predictions, and the clamped‑fillet configuration significantly reduced peak stresses and strains compared to the unclamped case. Across all mesh densities, the clamped‑fillet design maintained lower deformation and more favorable stress distribution while still producing the required strain‑gauge output. Based on these results, the clamped‑fillet wrench geometry was chosen as the final design due to its superior structural performance and reliability under load.
