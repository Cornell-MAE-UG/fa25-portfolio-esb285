## Project Overview

For the Mechanics of Materials (MAE 3270) course, the final project was to design a wrench that fulfilled a multitude of design criteria using hand calculations as well as ANSYS to do the analysis. The design needed to meet several safety factors while maintaining practical dimensions for real-world use.

## Hand Calculation Analysis

### Initial Design Considerations

Initial hand calculation diagrams were developed to understand the mechanical behavior:

![Hand-calculation Diagrams]({{ "/assets/images/actual_images_used/Ansys Images/fig1a.png" | relative_url }}){: style="width: 350px"}
![Hand-calculation Diagrams]({{ "/assets/images/actual_images_used/Ansys Images/fig1b.png" | relative_url }}){: style="width: 350px"}

Using the beam theory assumption, the design criteria included having at least a factor of safety against yield of 4, a factor of safety against crack growth of 2, a factor of safety against fatigue of 1.5, and a strain output of at least 1 mV/V.

### MATLAB Hand Calculation Script

```matlab
function hand=hand_calc(L, h, b)
    disp(strcat('L:', num2str(L), ', h:', num2str(h), ', b:', num2str(b)))
    M = 600; % max torque (in-lbf)
    c = 1.0; % distance from center of drive to center of strain gauge
    E = 9.86E6; % Young's modulus (psi)
    nu = 0.333; % Poisson's ratio
    su = 61.2E3; % tensile strength use yield or ultimate depending on material (psi)
    KIC = 24.6E3; % fracture toughness (psi sqrt(in))
    sfatigue = 30.e3; % fatigue strength from Granta for 10^6 cycles
    k=2;

    p=M/L;
    s=6*M/(b*h^2);
    s_0=s/10^3;
    X=su/s;
    disp(strcat('Max stress: ', num2str(s_0), 'psi'))
    disp(strcat('X_0: ', num2str(X)));
    disp(strcat('Does X_0 pass?_', num2str(X>=4)))


    %% Calculating Max Displacement
    I=(b*h^3)/12;
    displacement=(p*L^3)/(3*E*I);
    disp(strcat('Displacement: ', num2str(displacement), ' in'));

    %% Calculating strain
    st1=(s/E)*(L-c)/L;
    st2=-st1;
    st=k*(st1-st2)*(10^6)/4; % mV/V
    disp(strcat('Strain: ', num2str(st), ' microstrain'));
    disp(strcat('Output: ', num2str(st/1000), 'mV/V'))
    disp(strcat('Does strain pass?_', num2str(st/1000>=1)))

    %%Calculate factor of safety of crack growth
    a = 0.04; %crack depth (in) (0.01 mm)
    F=1.12;
    Sg = 6*M/(b*h^2);
    K = F*Sg*sqrt(pi*a);
    Xk=KIC/K;
    disp(strcat('X_k: ', num2str(Xk)));
    disp(strcat('Does X_k pass?_', num2str(Xk>=2)))

    %%Calculate factor of safety of fatigue stress
    Xs=sfatigue*b*h^2/(6*M);
    disp(strcat('X_s: ', num2str(Xs)));
    disp(strcat('Does X_s pass?_', num2str(Xs>=1.5)))
    disp('-----')

    end
hand_calc(9,0.8,0.5);
```

## Material Selection Analysis

### Material Properties

The material of this wrench was decided to be Aluminium-2424-T3. This aluminum has a Young's modulus of 9.86 Msi, a Poisson's ratio of 0.333, a tensile strength of 61.2 ksi, a fracture toughness of 24.6 ksi√in, and a fatigue strength of 30 ksi for 10^6 cycles.

### Material Verification Script

```matlab
function material=find_material(L, h, b)
    disp(strcat('L:', num2str(L), ', h:', num2str(h), ', b:', num2str(b)))
    M = 600; % max torque (in-lbf)
    c = 1.0; % distance from center of drive to center of strain gauge
    nu = 0.335; % Poisson's ratio
    k=2;

    X=4;
    s=6*M/(b*h^2);
    su = X*s;
    disp(strcat('Min su:  ', num2str(su), 'psi'));

    st=1;
    E=k*s*(L-c)*10^3/(2*st*L);
    disp(strcat('Max Youngs mod:  ', num2str(E), 'psi'));

    %%Calculate factor of safety of crack growth
    a = 0.04; %crack depth (in) (0.01 mm)
    Xk=2;
    F=1.12;
    Sg = 6*M/(b*h^2);
    K = F*Sg*sqrt(pi*a);
    KIC=Xk*K;
    disp(strcat('KIC min:  ', num2str(KIC)));

    %%Calculate factor of safety of fatigue stress
    Xs = 1.5;
    sfatigue=Xs*(6*M)/(b*h^2);
    disp(strcat('sfatigue min:  ', num2str(sfatigue)));
    disp('-----')


    end

find_material(9, 0.8, 0.5);
```

## ANSYS Finite Element Analysis

### Analysis Setup

Using ANSYS, the wrench was analysed using a load of 66.67 lbf applied horizontally a distance of 9 in from the end of the drive (to simulate a person applying force) and two different boundary conditions: unclamped and clamped fillets.

![ANSYS Setup - Load Application]({{ "/assets/images/actual_images_used/Ansys Images/fig3a.JPG" | relative_url }}){: style="width: 350px"}
![ANSYS Setup - Boundary Conditions]({{ "/assets/images/actual_images_used/Ansys Images/fig3b.JPG" | relative_url }}){: style="width: 350px"}

### Mesh Analysis

The analysis of the wrench was done with three different mesh conditions:

- **Coarse Mesh**: Wrench: 0.175 in, Drive: 0.1 in
- **Refined Mesh**: Wrench: 0.125 in, Drive: 0.06 in
- **Very Refined Mesh**: Wrench: 0.075 in, Drive: 0.03 in

### Strain Gauge Selection

The strain gauge selected to be used is SGT-1LH/350-TY43 by DWYEROMEGA:

![Strain Gauge Specifications]({{ "assets/images/actual_images_used/SGT_HALF-BRIDGE-UNIAXIAL_m.jpg" | relative_url }}){: style="width: 350px"}

This is a half-bridge strain gauge with a length of 0.362 in and a width of 0.157 in. It is made for Aluminum and would be located an inch from the drive.

### Key Findings

Overall, there is a trend of the maximum normal stress and strain increasing as the mesh is more refined. The maximum normal stress and strain also decrease from unclamped fillet condition to clamped fillet condition, indicating that the clamped fillet design provides superior structural performance and better stress distribution.

The clamped fillet design was selected as the final design due to its superior performance in reducing stress concentrations and limiting deformation under load.
